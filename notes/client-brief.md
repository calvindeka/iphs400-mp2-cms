# Client brief

**Client:** Knox County Historical Society (course default, with added details — see note at bottom).

**Who runs the site, and how comfortable are they with computers?**
Three volunteers. Karen, the secretary, posts meeting announcements and event notices weekly; she's comfortable with email and not much else — no patience for anything that looks like "software." Tom, the president, writes longer research pieces every month or two and wants them to read as permanent, citable work, not throwaway posts. Priya, the treasurer, wants a simple page listing how to pay dues, but actively avoids anything labeled "admin" — she'll use it if it looks like a form, not a dashboard.

**Who reads it, and what are they usually trying to find?**
Members and prospective members. The three things people ask about every month, without fail: when is the next meeting, where is it, and what's on the agenda. A smaller group reads Tom's research pieces and wants to find them again months later — those need permanent-feeling URLs, not something buried in a feed.

**What is broken about how they do it now?**
Their current site is a Facebook page. It isn't searchable, so nobody can find Tom's older pieces or last quarter's meeting notes. Event locations sometimes need to stay off the page until close to the date (the group had a scare with an unwelcome visitor showing up at a meeting location listed too far in advance), and there's no way to control that on Facebook — everything is public and permanent the moment it posts. Tom also doesn't trust any tool that reformats his writing: pasting from Word into Facebook has mangled his paragraphs before, so he re-checks everything by hand now.

**What would make them say "this is better"?**
Karen can post a meeting announcement in under two minutes without help. Tom's research pieces keep their formatting and get a permanent, memorable URL he can hand out. Priya can update the dues page without anyone calling it "admin work." And the three recurring questions (when/where/agenda) are answerable from the homepage without scrolling a feed.

**What must not happen?**
- Nobody but Tom edits or deletes the membership/leadership content.
- An event's exact address must not appear on the public site until 48 hours before the event (the earlier scare drives this — it's a hard rule, not a preference).
- A draft (an unfinished research piece, a not-yet-final agenda) must never be visible to the public, even briefly.
